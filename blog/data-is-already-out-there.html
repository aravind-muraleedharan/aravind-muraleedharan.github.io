Your data is already out there 🌐

AI makes that a bigger deal. Being safe is now a choice, not a default.

Aravind M · Nov 2026 · 4 min read

In 3 lines

  • Much of your personal data is already online, often shared by others, not you.
  • AI can remember, reason over and reform that data at scale.
  • You can’t opt out of everything, but you can opt out of some convenience.

The problem: your data is already out there

Think about what’s online about you right now. Not just what you posted. Your photos sit in other people’s albums and group chats. Your phone number lives in hundreds of contact lists. And think about what you’ve handed over without a second thought:

  • Your resume, sent to recruiters and job portals.
  • Aadhaar and PAN copies shared on WhatsApp to get an application processed.
  • Income and bank details for a loan enquiry.
  • Your face or fingerprint, scanned every day at the office punching machine.

You didn’t share all of it yourself. Somebody did, somewhere along the way.

The internet makes this worse, because the road runs both ways. We stay logged in to Gmail, keep search history switched on, save bank passwords in the browser and let apps read our OTPs. Each of these makes life easier. Each one is also one more place your data can leak from. If it’s connected to the internet, it’s not fully safe.

Why it matters now: AI 🤖

Leaked data used to be scattered. Joining the dots took time and effort. AI changes that. It can:

  • Remember: hold huge amounts of information and recall it instantly.
  • Reason: learn patterns and connect scattered details into a picture of who you are, where you go and what you’re likely to do.
  • Reform: turn it into something new. A convincing message in your manager’s tone, a cloned voice, a fake profile built from your photos.

In the agentic AI era, it doesn’t stop at thinking. AI can now make decisions and act on its own, and the closer it gets to general intelligence, the more complicated this becomes.

But AI is already part of daily life, and we can’t run away from it (olichottam isn’t an option, as we’d say in Malayalam). What we can do is be choosy.

What you can do

My view: we don’t need to be worried, but we do need to be careful. Everyone is making AI versions of their photos these days, and you might want to as well. That’s fine. Just don’t also use your face as the key to your phone or your home. Spend your risk where it gives you joy, and save it elsewhere.

Most of the risk we take on is for ease, and ease is optional. Here are the swaps I think are worth making:

ConvenienceSafer swapWhy
💳 Tap to payUse your PINA lost card can be tapped without it. The extra 1–2 seconds is worth it.
🙂 Face or fingerprint unlockPIN, password or keyYou can change a PIN. You can’t change your face.
🔓 PIN-less paymentsTurn off, or set a low limitSmall payments add up before you notice.
📩 Auto-read OTPsType OTPs yourselfHandy, until the wrong app can read them too.
🔑 Saved bank passwordsDon’t save themOne breach exposes them all.

(Yes, a thief can still break a lock. That’s a different blog.)

Two more habits: share ID documents only through official channels when they’re really needed, and treat any message that asks for urgency, money or codes as fake until proven otherwise.

Being smart doesn’t mean using every tool you have. Being safe is a choice.
